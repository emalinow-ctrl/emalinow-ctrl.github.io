veilaudit

Catch the faces that
blur was supposed to hide.

Veilaudit is an open-source, fully local pipeline that audits folders of video for improperly censored faces. Redaction failures are invisible until they're a scandal — this finds them before publication, frame by frame, with a review queue and an auditable report.

Why it matters

Redaction fails silently

A blur a human can still read, a black box over a PDF's text layer, a pixelated face that embedding search still identifies. Failures ship because nothing checks them.

Audits, not just detection

The output isn't a guess — it's a per-frame, per-track report you can act on: every face judged sharp after it should have been hidden.

Runs on your machine

No cloud APIs, no uploads, no content filters. Footage never leaves the machine it's on.

How it works

  1. Decode — samples every Nth frame of each video via OpenCV/FFmpeg.
  2. Detect — SCRFD face detection (ONNX Runtime), run at your chosen size.
  3. Judge — Laplacian variance, FFT high-frequency ratio, edge density, and a solid-mask (black bar) check, on the full-resolution crop.
  4. Track — IoU tracking gives every face a stable ID and a vote history, so one ambiguous frame can't flag a person — and one frame can't hide them.
  5. Review — flagged frames land in a review queue with a dashboard; a learning loop tunes thresholds from your accept/deny labels.
A learned classifier (MobileNetV3-Small, trained on synthesized censorship — blur, mosaic, black bars — exported to ONNX) is included as a tiebreaker for the hand-tuned metrics.

Status

Pre-alpha, under active development. Roadmap: harden the video benchmark suite first, then extend the same audit contract to documents (redacted-PDF audits) and audio (de-anonymization checks). Apache-2.0.
